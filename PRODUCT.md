# Lost & Found

A cooperative browser detective game for two to four friends or family members,
at a table or talking over a call. Each person uses an independent browser and
contributes a different investigative perspective. An anonymous browser cookie
holds a persistent seat; nicknames label people but never authorise access.

## Main case: After Hours

Four procedural miniature rooms, sixteen authored records, seven evidence-backed
deductions and a six-event reconstruction. Three acts turn an apparently obvious
collection into a question about two separate journeys. The target is 20–30
minutes; no human duration or enjoyment result has yet been recorded.

Inspect objects, share useful findings, compare records, test an inference and
open the next room. Three optional hint levels support discussion without a
countdown or score penalty. Two people cover two duties each; third and fourth
arrivals divide the work. Duties can be transferred explicitly so a departure
does not permanently block the investigation. There is no special live host.

## Classic case

The Blue Hour remains a short solo/two-person introduction, including all prior
saved investigations and its original eight clues, timeline and conclusion.
Earlier crit history and cutoff tags remain unchanged. New rooms pin their case
version and scenery seed; a deployment does not reinterpret an old investigation.

## Quality claims
- Another perspective supplies evidence needed to establish a deduction.
- An unsupported correct guess cannot advance the main case.
- Private findings remain with their duty until explicitly shared.
- All object actions and facts are available as text, using the same permissions
  and discovery steps as 3D. No answer depends on pixel hunting or hearing.
- A saved action is acknowledged by the database; shared changes arrive without
  a reload. Stale rearrangements and duty transfers are rejected explicitly.
- Rooms survive reconnects, process restarts and redeployments on the same volume.
- Procedural decoration is not procedural truth or a claim of endless replay.

Technical checks can enforce these contracts. Real first-time players must still
judge comprehensibility, equitable participation, satisfying inference and pace.
The student reviews and owns the final personal and research arguments.
